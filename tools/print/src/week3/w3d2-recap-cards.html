Definition1
What is a green skill? Give two examples.
AnswerA skill that directly combats the effects of climate change by reducing pollution or preserving natural resources — e.g. water preservation, energy efficiency. Hyman and Smith (2023)
Skills gap2
What is the skills gap in the LinkedIn report?
AnswerDemand for green skills is growing faster than the supply of workers who have them: only one in eight workers has green skills. Hyman and Smith (2023)
Cause → solution3
What causes the skills gap, and how can education close it?
AnswerJobs change fast because of green policies; skills develop more slowly. Fix: targeted training, updated courses, continuous and on-the-job learning. Bowen et al. (2018) · Hyman and Smith (2023)
Greening roles4
Name three jobs that don’t look green but are “greening”. Why?
AnswerFleet managers, compliance managers, health workers, data scientists — all sectors now face environmental rules and expectations. Hyman and Smith (2023)
The future5
What does the LinkedIn report predict about demand for green skills?
AnswerIt will keep rising: jobs needing a green skill grew about 9% a year for five years, with a 20% jump last year. Hyman and Smith (2023)
Numbers6
What does the number 22.4% refer to?
AnswerThe share of job postings in the last year that required at least one green skill. Hyman and Smith (2023)
Numbers7
How much more likely are workers with green skills to get hired?
Answer30% more likely than workers without green skills. Hyman and Smith (2023)
Key term8
What is “skill distance”? Give an example.
AnswerHow much re-training you need to move from one job to another — small from marketing manager to sales manager, large to software developer. Bowen et al. (2018)
Compare9
How are green occupations different from non-green ones?
AnswerLess routine-intensive and higher-skill; they need high-level analytical and technical skills, more education and on-the-job training. Bowen et al. (2018)
Your view10
Is upskilling in green skills a good idea for you? Give one reason and one source.
